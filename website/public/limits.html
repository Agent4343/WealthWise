🏦 TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account

Contributions are not tax-deductible, but all growth and withdrawals are completely tax-free. Forever.

2026 Annual Limit $7,000
Cumulative Room (since 2009, age 18+) $102,000
Over-Contribution Penalty 1%/month
Withdrawal Room Restored Jan 1 next year

Key Insight: 57% of Canadian TFSAs are held entirely in cash or GICs. A TFSA is not a savings account — it's a tax-free investment container. Fill it with low-cost index ETFs.

📊 RRSP — Registered Retirement Savings Plan

Contributions are tax-deductible. Growth is tax-deferred. Withdrawals are taxed as income in retirement.

2026 Maximum Contribution $33,810
2025 Maximum Contribution $32,490
Contribution Formula 18% of prior year income
Unused Room Carries forward indefinitely
Over-Contribution Buffer $2,000 (no penalty)
2025 Tax Year Deadline March 2, 2026
Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) $60,000 max withdrawal
Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) $20,000 max withdrawal

🏠 FHSA — First Home Savings Account

The best of both worlds: tax-deductible contributions (like RRSP) + tax-free withdrawals for a first home (like TFSA).

2026 Annual Limit $8,000
Lifetime Limit $40,000
Carry-Forward (unused room) Up to $8,000/year
Maximum Annual Contribution $16,000 (with carry-forward)
Account Lifespan 15 years (or age 71)
Eligibility Canadian resident, 18+, first-time buyer
Tax Treatment Deductible in + Tax-free out
The FHSA is the most tax-efficient account in Canada for first-time home buyers. You get the RRSP-style deduction when you contribute, and TFSA-style tax-free growth and withdrawal when you buy your first home. If you don't buy, funds can transfer to your RRSP tax-free.

🎓 RESP — Registered Education Savings Plan

Save for a child's post-secondary education. The government matches 20% of your contributions through the CESG.

Annual Contribution Limit No annual cap
Lifetime Limit (per child) $50,000
CESG Match Rate 20% on first $2,500/year
CESG Annual Maximum $500/year
CESG Lifetime Maximum $7,200 per child
CESG Carry-Forward Up to $1,000/year (if missed)
Additional CESG (low income) Extra 10-20% on first $500
CLB (Canada Learning Bond) Up to $2,000 (low income)
To maximize the CESG, contribute $2,500/year per child. The government adds $500 (20% match) — that's an instant 20% return before any investment growth. Over 18 years, the CESG alone adds $7,200 per child.

🍁 CPP — Canada Pension Plan

Mandatory contributions from employment income. Both you and your employer contribute.

2025 Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE) $71,300
2025 Second Ceiling (YAMPE — CPP2) $81,200
Basic Exemption $3,500
CPP1 Employee Rate 5.95%
CPP2 Employee Rate 4%
Max CPP1 Employee Contribution $4,034.10
Max Monthly CPP Retirement (age 65) ~$1,364/month
Average Monthly CPP Payment ~$815/month
CPP alone won't fund your retirement. The maximum monthly CPP payment at age 65 is ~$1,364 — and most Canadians receive far less. You need your own RRSP, TFSA, and investment portfolio to bridge the gap.
